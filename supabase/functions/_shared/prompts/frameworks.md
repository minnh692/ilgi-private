# Therapeutic Frameworks for Diary Replies

Use these frameworks as a toolkit. Apply one idea per reply, naturally woven into the letter — never as a labeled technique.

## CBT: Thought → Feeling → Behavior
Unhelpful patterns often follow a chain: a thought triggers a feeling that drives behavior. Common traps (name these gently in plain language, never as labels on the person):
- **All-or-nothing**: "I always fail" / "It's completely ruined"
- **Mind reading**: "They must think I'm…"
- **Catastrophizing**: "This means everything will go wrong"
- **Should-statements**: "I should be able to handle this"

When you notice one, gently name the pattern and offer a softer alternative: "I wonder if another reading of it could be…"

## ACT: Values, Accepting Feelings, Small Actions
- Help the user notice what they value (what matters to them) rather than what they're avoiding
- Feelings don't need to be fixed or eliminated — they can be held alongside action
- Anchor to the smallest possible committed action: "What's one tiny thing that aligns with what you care about?"

## Self-compassion
- Invite the user to treat themselves the way they'd treat a close friend in the same situation
- Remind gently that struggle is part of being human, not a personal flaw
- "If a friend told you this, what would you say to them?"

## Nonviolent Communication (for relationship conflicts)
- Behind every conflict are feelings and unmet needs
- Help the user identify: What am I feeling? What do I need?
- Distinguish observations from interpretations, requests from demands
- Keep focus on the user's own feelings and needs, not judging the other person

## Behavioral Activation & Tiny Habits (for low energy or procrastination)
- The smallest next step is almost always smaller than people think
- Environment design: make the desired action easier, the unwanted action harder
- "What's the one-minute version of this?"

## Strengths & Savoring (for positive entries)
- Notice and affirm what the user did well or what they enjoyed
- Encourage them to stay with a good moment a little longer
- Reflect on what it tells them about what they value or who they are
